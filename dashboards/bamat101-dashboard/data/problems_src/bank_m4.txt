### TOPIC O | 4 | Complementary Function (Homogeneous Case)
#### O1 | Remember | 13 | 27
Q:
State the three root cases of the auxiliary equation of \(ay''+by'+cy=0\) and the form of the complementary function for each.
A:
Real distinct roots \(m_1\neq m_2\): \(y=C_1e^{m_1x}+C_2e^{m_2x}\). Repeated root \(m\): \(y=(C_1+C_2x)e^{mx}\). Complex roots \(\alpha\pm i\beta\): \(y=e^{\alpha x}(C_1\cos\beta x+C_2\sin\beta x)\).
#### O2 | Apply | 13 | 27
Q:
Solve \(y''-7y'+12y=0\).
A:
\(m=3,4\): \(y=C_1e^{3x}+C_2e^{4x}\).
#### O3 | Apply | 13 | 27
Q:
Solve \(y''+6y'+9y=0\).
A:
\((m+3)^2=0\): \(y=(C_1+C_2x)e^{-3x}\).
#### O4 | Apply | 13 | 27
Q:
Solve \(y''-4y'+13y=0\).
A:
\(m=2\pm3i\): \(y=e^{2x}(C_1\cos3x+C_2\sin3x)\).
#### O5 | Analyze | 13 | 27
Q:
Using the discriminant, classify each as oscillatory, purely exponential, or repeated-root: (a) \(m^2+4m+4=0\), (b) \(m^2+4=0\), (c) \(m^2+5m+6=0\).
A:
(a) discriminant \(0\): repeated root, \(x\)-multiplied term. (b) discriminant \(<0\): complex roots, oscillatory. (c) discriminant \(>0\): distinct real roots, purely exponential.
#### O6 | Evaluate | 13 | 27
Q:
A student leaves the solution of \(y''+2y'+5y=0\) as \(C_1e^{(-1+2i)x}+C_2e^{(-1-2i)x}\). Is this acceptable, and why is the real form preferred?
A:
Mathematically equivalent (with complex constants), but the expected general real solution is \(e^{-x}(C_1\cos2x+C_2\sin2x)\): it needs only real constants and displays the decay and oscillation directly.
### TOPIC PI | 4 | Undetermined Coefficients and Resonance
#### PI1 | Remember | 13 | 27
Q:
State the trial form for the particular integral when \(R(x)\) is (a) an exponential, (b) a degree-\(n\) polynomial, (c) a sine or cosine, and when it must be modified.
A:
(a) \(e^{kx}\): \(Ae^{kx}\). (b) polynomial: a full degree-\(n\) polynomial. (c) \(\sin kx,\cos kx\): \(A\cos kx+B\sin kx\). Multiply by \(x\) (or \(x^2\)) whenever the guess already appears in the CF — resonance.
#### PI2 | Apply | 13 | 27
Q:
Find the particular integral of \(y''-3y'+2y=e^{3x}\).
A:
Try \(Ae^{3x}\): \((9-9+2)A=1\), so \(y_{PI}=\tfrac12e^{3x}\).
#### PI3 | Apply | 13 | 27
Q:
Solve \(y''+4y=8x^2\) completely.
A:
\(y=C_1\cos2x+C_2\sin2x+2x^2-1\).
#### PI4 | Apply | 13 | 27
Q:
(Resonance) Solve \(y''-4y'+4y=e^{2x}\), noting the naive guess collides with a repeated root.
A:
CF \(=(C_1+C_2x)e^{2x}\), so try \(Ax^2e^{2x}\): substitution gives \(2A=1\). \(y=(C_1+C_2x)e^{2x}+\tfrac12x^2e^{2x}\).
#### PI5 | Analyze | 13 | 27
Q:
For \(y''-y'-2y=3e^{2x}+\sin x\) (roots \(2,-1\)), decide for each term whether resonance occurs and give the trial forms.
A:
\(3e^{2x}\): \(m=2\) is a root, so resonance, try \(Axe^{2x}\). \(\sin x\): no root matches \(\pm i\), so try \(A\cos x+B\sin x\). The full PI is the sum.
#### PI6 | Evaluate | 13 | 27
Q:
A student solving \(y''+y=\sin x\) with the guess \(A\cos x+B\sin x\) gets \(0=\sin x\). What went wrong, and what is the correct guess?
A:
The roots are \(\pm i\), so \(\sin x\) is already in the CF — resonance. Correct trial: \(y_{PI}=x(A\cos x+B\sin x)\).
#### PI7 | Create | 13 | 27
Q:
Construct a second-order ODE with real distinct roots and initial conditions so the solution is a sum of two exponentials with positive coefficients; solve it.
A:
E.g. \(y''-3y'+2y=0,\ y(0)=2,\ y'(0)=3\): \(y=C_1e^x+C_2e^{2x}\) with \(C_1=C_2=1\), so \(y=e^x+e^{2x}\).
### TOPIC VP | 4 | Method of Variation of Parameters
#### VP1 | Remember | 13 | 28
Q:
State the variation-of-parameters formula for \(y''+Py'+Qy=R\) and when it is preferred over undetermined coefficients.
A:
With CF solutions \(y_1,y_2\) and Wronskian \(W=y_1y_2'-y_1'y_2\):
\[ y_p=-y_1\int\frac{y_2R}{W}\,dx+y_2\int\frac{y_1R}{W}\,dx. \]
Preferred when \(R(x)\) is not of the standard trial form (\(\tan x,\ \sec x,\ 1/x,\ e^x/x,\dots\)).
#### VP2 | Apply | 13 | 28
Q:
Solve \(y''+y=\sec x\).
A:
\(y_1=\cos x,\ y_2=\sin x,\ W=1\). \(y_p=-\cos x\int\tan x\,dx+\sin x\int dx=\cos x\ln|\cos x|+x\sin x\). \(y=C_1\cos x+C_2\sin x+\cos x\ln|\cos x|+x\sin x\).
#### VP3 | Apply | 13 | 28
Q:
Solve \(y''-2y'+y=\dfrac{e^x}{x}\).
A:
\(y_1=e^x,\ y_2=xe^x,\ W=e^{2x}\). \(y_p=-e^x\int1\,dx+xe^x\int\frac1x\,dx=-xe^x+xe^x\ln x\). The term \(-xe^x\) is absorbed into the CF, so \(y=(C_1+C_2x)e^x+xe^x\ln x\).
#### VP4 | Apply | 13 | 28
Q:
Find a particular integral of \(y''+4y=\tan2x\).
A:
\(y_1=\cos2x,\ y_2=\sin2x,\ W=2\). After integrating, \(y_p=-\tfrac14\cos2x\,\ln|\sec2x+\tan2x|\) (the \(\sin2x\cos2x\) terms cancel).
#### VP5 | Analyze | 14 | 28
Q:
Why does undetermined coefficients fail for \(y''+y=\tan x\), while variation of parameters works?
A:
Repeated differentiation of \(\tan x\) keeps producing new independent functions (\(\sec^2x,\ \sec^2x\tan x,\dots\)) so no finite trial family exists. Variation of parameters needs only the CF solutions and integrals, whatever \(R(x)\) is.
#### VP6 | Evaluate | 14 | 28
Q:
A student finds \(y_p=-xe^x+xe^x\ln x\) for \(y''-2y'+y=e^x/x\) and calls it wrong because \(-xe^x\) lies in the CF. Evaluate.
A:
Not wrong. A particular integral is not unique; \(-xe^x\) is a CF term and is absorbed into \(C_2xe^x\). The general solution is unchanged.
#### VP7 | Create | 14 | 28
Q:
Construct a constant-coefficient ODE that requires variation of parameters, and set up (without solving) the two integrals.
A:
E.g. \(y''+9y=\sec3x\): \(y_1=\cos3x,\ y_2=\sin3x,\ W=3\), so
\[ y_p=-\cos3x\int\frac{\sin3x\sec3x}{3}\,dx+\sin3x\int\frac{\cos3x\sec3x}{3}\,dx. \]
### TOPIC CE | 4 | Cauchy–Euler Equation
#### CE1 | Remember | 14 | 28
Q:
State the Cauchy–Euler form, the substitution that solves it, and the resulting auxiliary equation.
A:
\(x^2y''+axy'+by=R(x)\). Put \(x=e^t\) (\(t=\ln x\)), \(D=\frac{d}{dt}\): \(xy'=Dy,\ x^2y''=D(D-1)y\). Homogeneous case: try \(y=x^m\), giving \(m(m-1)+am+b=0\).
#### CE2 | Apply | 14 | 28
Q:
Solve \(x^2y''-2xy'-4y=0\).
A:
\(m(m-1)-2m-4=m^2-3m-4=0,\ m=4,-1\): \(y=C_1x^4+C_2x^{-1}\).
#### CE3 | Apply | 14 | 28
Q:
Solve \(x^2y''+3xy'+y=0\).
A:
\(m^2+2m+1=0\): \(m=-1\) repeated, \(y=(C_1+C_2\ln x)\dfrac1x\).
#### CE4 | Apply | 14 | 28
Q:
Solve \(x^2y''+xy'+y=0\).
A:
\(m^2+1=0,\ m=\pm i\): \(y=C_1\cos(\ln x)+C_2\sin(\ln x)\).
#### CE5 | Apply | 14 | 28
Q:
Solve \(x^2y''-3xy'+4y=x^2\).
A:
With \(x=e^t\): \((D-2)^2y=e^{2t}\). CF \(=(C_1+C_2t)e^{2t}\); resonance gives PI \(=\tfrac12t^2e^{2t}\). So \(y=x^2(C_1+C_2\ln x)+\tfrac12x^2(\ln x)^2\).
#### CE6 | Analyze | 14 | 28
Q:
Show that \(x=e^t\) gives \(xy'=\dfrac{dy}{dt}\) and \(x^2y''=\dfrac{d^2y}{dt^2}-\dfrac{dy}{dt}\), and say why this makes the coefficients constant.
A:
\(t=\ln x\Rightarrow\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac1x\dfrac{dy}{dt}\), so \(xy'=y_t\). Differentiating again: \(y''=-\dfrac{1}{x^2}y_t+\dfrac{1}{x^2}y_{tt}\), so \(x^2y''=y_{tt}-y_t\). Each power \(x^ky^{(k)}\) becomes a constant-coefficient operator in \(t\).
#### CE7 | Evaluate | 14 | 28
Q:
A student solves \(x^2y''+xy'-4y=0\) by assuming \(y=e^{mx}\). Evaluate, and give the right approach.
A:
Wrong: the coefficients are variable, so \(e^{mx}\) gives no common factor to cancel. Use \(y=x^m\): \(m^2-4=0\), so \(y=C_1x^2+C_2x^{-2}\).
#### CE8 | Create | 14 | 28
Q:
Construct a Cauchy–Euler equation whose general solution is \(y=C_1x^3+C_2x^{-2}\).
A:
Need \(m=3,-2\): \(m^2-m-6=0=m(m-1)+am+b\) with \(a=0,\ b=-6\). So \(x^2y''-6y=0\).
### TOPIC CL | 4 | Cauchy–Legendre Equation
#### CL1 | Remember | 14 | 28
Q:
State the Cauchy–Legendre form and its substitution; how does it relate to Cauchy–Euler?
A:
\((ax+b)^2y''+A(ax+b)y'+By=R(x)\). Put \(ax+b=e^t\): \((ax+b)y'=a\,Dy\) and \((ax+b)^2y''=a^2D(D-1)y\). Cauchy–Euler is the special case \(a=1,\ b=0\).
#### CL2 | Apply | 14 | 29
Q:
Solve \((1+x)^2y''+(1+x)y'+y=0\).
A:
Put \(1+x=e^t\): \(D(D-1)+D+1=D^2+1\), so \(y=C_1\cos\ln(1+x)+C_2\sin\ln(1+x)\).
#### CL3 | Apply | 14 | 29
Q:
Solve \((2x+3)^2y''-2(2x+3)y'-12y=6x\).
A:
Put \(2x+3=e^t\): \(4(D^2-2D-3)y=3e^t-9\). CF \(=C_1e^{3t}+C_2e^{-t}\); PI \(=-\tfrac{3}{16}e^t+\tfrac34\). So \(y=C_1(2x+3)^3+C_2(2x+3)^{-1}-\tfrac38x+\tfrac{3}{16}\).
#### CL4 | Apply | 14 | 29
Q:
Solve \((3x+2)^2y''+3(3x+2)y'-36y=0\).
A:
Put \(3x+2=e^t\): \(9D(D-1)+9D-36=9(D^2-4)\), roots \(\pm2\). \(y=C_1(3x+2)^2+C_2(3x+2)^{-2}\).
#### CL5 | Evaluate | 14 | 29
Q:
A student tries \(y=x^m\) on \((x+1)^2y''+(x+1)y'-y=0\). Evaluate and give the correct trial.
A:
Wrong shift: the equation is in powers of \((x+1)\), so the trial is \(y=(x+1)^m\): \(m^2-1=0\), giving \(y=C_1(x+1)+C_2(x+1)^{-1}\).
#### CL6 | Create | 14 | 29
Q:
Construct a Cauchy–Legendre equation in \((2x+1)\) whose general solution is \(y=C_1(2x+1)^2+C_2(2x+1)^{-1}\).
A:
Need roots \(2,-1\), i.e. \(D^2-D-2\). With \(2x+1=e^t\) (so \((2x+1)^2y''=4D(D-1)y\)): \((2x+1)^2y''-8y=0\). Check: \(y=(2x+1)^2\) has \(y''=8\) and \(8(2x+1)^2-8(2x+1)^2=0\).
### TOPIC AP | 4 | Applications of Linear Second-Order ODEs
#### AP1 | Remember | 15 | 29
Q:
State the ODEs for a damped spring–mass system and a series LCR circuit, and the three damping regimes.
A:
Spring–mass: \(mx''+cx'+kx=F(t)\). LCR: \(Lq''+Rq'+\dfrac qC=E(t)\). Regimes by \(c^2-4mk\): \(>0\) overdamped (two real roots), \(=0\) critically damped (repeated root), \(<0\) underdamped (oscillatory decay).
#### AP2 | Apply | 15 | 29
Q:
A \(0.5\) kg mass on a spring with \(k=8\) N/m is pulled \(0.3\) m and released from rest (no damping). Find \(x(t)\), the amplitude and the period.
A:
\(x''+16x=0,\ \omega=4\): \(x=0.3\cos4t\). Amplitude \(0.3\) m, period \(2\pi/4=\pi/2\) s.
#### AP3 | Apply | 15 | 29
Q:
Solve \(x''+6x'+9x=0,\ x(0)=1,\ x'(0)=0\) and classify the damping.
A:
Repeated root \(-3\): critically damped. \(x=(C_1+C_2t)e^{-3t}\); \(C_1=1,\ C_2-3=0\), so \(x=(1+3t)e^{-3t}\).
#### AP4 | Apply | 15 | 29
Q:
A series LCR circuit has \(L=1\) H, \(R=4\ \Omega\), \(C=\tfrac15\) F, a constant EMF \(E=10\) V, and starts with \(q(0)=0,\ i(0)=0\). Find \(q(t)\).
A:
\(q''+4q'+5q=10\); roots \(-2\pm i\), \(q_p=2\). Initial conditions give \(q=2-e^{-2t}(2\cos t+4\sin t)\). The charge tends to \(2\) C.
#### AP5 | Analyze | 15 | 29
Q:
Solve \(x''+4x=\cos2t\) and describe what happens physically.
A:
The driving frequency equals the natural frequency \(\omega_0=2\) — resonance. \(x_p=\dfrac t4\sin2t\), whose amplitude grows without bound (linearly in \(t\)).
#### AP6 | Evaluate | 15 | 29
Q:
A student says that because \(x''+2x'+5x=0\) has complex roots, the system "oscillates forever". Evaluate.
A:
Roots \(-1\pm2i\) have a negative real part: \(x=e^{-t}(\cdots)\) oscillates with decaying amplitude (underdamped). Oscillation forever occurs only with no damping (\(c=0\)).
#### AP7 | Create | 15 | 29
Q:
Choose the damping coefficient \(c\) for \(m=1,\ k=4\) so the system is critically damped, and give the general solution.
A:
Need \(c^2=4mk=16\), so \(c=4\): \(x''+4x'+4x=0\), giving \(x=(C_1+C_2t)e^{-2t}\).
