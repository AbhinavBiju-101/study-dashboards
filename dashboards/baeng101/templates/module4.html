11Types of Review

A review paper reports no new experiment; it synthesises what is already known, like a cheat sheet for a research field. Knowing the type changes everything about how you write it, and the exam asks you to identify and justify.

Skeleton of a review paper
SectionWhat it does
Title & abstractHook the reader, summarise scope
IntroductionWhy this review? What gap does it fill?
Body / thematic sectionsOrganised synthesis of the literature
DiscussionPatterns, contradictions, insights
ConclusionWhat we know and what is still unknown
ReferencesEvery source, properly cited

1.2 Narrative and systematic reviews

NarrativeThe storyteller: broad, flexible, author-driven. Strength: accessible overview. Weakness: author choice means potential bias and it cannot be reproduced.
SystematicThe scientist: strict and reproducible, the gold standard in engineering. Four stages: Identification (search databases) → Screening (title and abstract) → Eligibility (inclusion/exclusion criteria) → Included (final studies).
Misconception"Systematic just means more thorough." It means the method is fixed in advance and reproducible: registered protocol, set criteria, independent screening, reasons for exclusion reported.

1.3 Meta-analysis and scoping reviews

Meta-analysisThe statistician: (1) collect comparable studies, (2) pool the data statistically, (3) calculate a combined effect size, turning many findings into one quantitative answer.
ScopingThe explorer: maps how much research exists, of what kinds, and where the gaps are. It reports counts and categories, not findings, and does not assess study quality. Territory can be known, emerging, or a gap.

1.4 Critical review

CriticalThe judge: evaluates the quality, significance and validity of the literature; the point is the judgement, not the coverage. Strength: shows depth. Weakness: highly subjective, needs strong expertise.

1.5 How to classify an extract

  1. Protocol registered, fixed criteria, independent screening? → Systematic (if numbers are also pooled statistically → Meta-analysis)
  2. Does it judge the quality and validity of studies? → Critical
  3. Does it count and categorise what exists (countries, designs) and say it did not assess quality? → Scoping
  4. Otherwise: author-selected, broad, no stated search method → Narrative
Worked example (original)"Two authors searched four databases using terms fixed beforehand. From 900 records, 40 met the pre-set criteria. The authors combined the effect sizes to estimate an overall reduction in error rate." Type: meta-analysis (a systematic method plus pooling). Scope: focused on one question. Method: fixed terms, pre-set criteria, effect sizes combined ("combined the effect sizes" is the giveaway). Best for: a single quantitative answer drawn from many studies. Every mark-bearing part (scope, method, best-for) is named.

2The Literature Review

Why it mattersIt shows what exists and how it relates to your question, finds the gap, and builds credibility by showing you know the field before entering it.
Misconception"A literature review is a summary of papers, one paragraph each." It is a synthesis by theme that ends by locating the gap your work fills.
Same sources, two versionsSummary (weak): Rao found X. Menon found Y. Iyer found Z.
Synthesis (strong): Two studies report X and Y (Rao, 2022; Menon, 2023), whereas Iyer (2024) finds Z; none tests it in general wards, so the gap is there.

Five steps: define a specific scope → search (IEEE Xplore, Google Scholar, PubMed) → evaluate (peer-reviewed, recent) → synthesise by theme → write and cite in one style.

33Referencing: APA and IEEE

Plagiarism means an automatic zero and permanent damage to your record; proper citation is your badge of academic honour. Five styles exist (APA, IEEE, MLA, Chicago, Harvard), each with its own rules. In this course you produce only APA and IEEE, for journal articles and books.

StyleHome fieldIn-text
APASocial sciences, education (author-date)(Smith, 2023)
IEEEEngineering, computing (numbered)[1]
MLAHumanities (author-page)(Smith 45)
ChicagoHistory, arts (footnotes or author-date)(Smith 2023, 45)
HarvardSciences, UK/Australia (like APA)(Smith, 2023, p. 45)

3.1 Reference list vs bibliography, and why styles differ

Reference listBibliography
IncludesOnly sources cited in the textAll sources consulted
Used byAPA, IEEE, Harvard, MLAChicago notes-bibliography
Why one style per documentA consistent format lets readers find year, volume and pages instantly, and indexing services can only count citations that follow a predictable shape. Mixing styles defeats both.

3.2 One source, five styles

StyleBook
APASmith, J. A. (2023). Research methods in engineering. Oxford University Press.
IEEE[1] J. A. Smith, Research Methods in Engineering. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2023.
MLASmith, John A. Research Methods in Engineering. Oxford University Press, 2023.
ChicagoSmith, John A. Research Methods in Engineering. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2023.

Only order, punctuation and italics change; the facts stay the same.

3.3 APA vs IEEE, rule by rule (journal article and book)

Exam scope: journal articles and books only, APA and IEEE only.

APA: Rao, K., & Menon, P. (2025). Campus energy use. Energy Studies, 14(2), 55–70.
IEEE: [1] K. Rao and P. Menon, “Campus energy use,” Energy Studies, vol. 14, no. 2, pp. 55–70, 2025.

Same colour = same fact. Watch it move: the year goes second in APA, last in IEEE; the pages gain "pp." in IEEE.

RuleAPAIEEE

Article titleSentence case, no quotes“Quotes,” comma inside
JournalItalic, title case; volume italicItalic, then comma
Vol/issue14(2)vol. 14, no. 2
Pages55–70pp. 55–70
AuthorsAPAIEEE
1Rao, K.K. Rao,
2Rao, K., & Menon, P.K. Rao and P. Menon,
3Rao, K., Menon, P., & Das, S.K. Rao, P. Menon, and S. Das,
Why the differences existAPA is author-date: readers look the year up in the text, so it sits beside the name. IEEE is numbered, so the source is found by number and the year can sit at the end. The format follows how the reader finds the entry.
Worked conversion (book, APA → IEEE)APA: Iqbal, A. (2023). Urban heat design. Kestrel Press.
1. Author → initial first: A. Iqbal,
2. Title in italic title case: Urban Heat Design.
3. Add place; publisher; year last.
IEEE: [1] A. Iqbal, Urban Heat Design. Chennai, India: Kestrel Press, 2023.
Self-check: initials first ✔ year last ✔ place added ✔
Spot the errors (IEEE)[1] Rao, K. and Menon, P., “Campus energy use,” Energy Studies, 14(2), 55–70, 2025. Errors: author order; "14(2)" is APA style; missing "pp.". Fixed: [1] K. Rao and P. Menon, “Campus energy use,” Energy Studies, vol. 14, no. 2, pp. 55–70, 2025.

Not examined: in-text citation, MLA, Chicago, Harvard.

3.4 Recognising the other three, and mistakes that cost marks

EntryStyleGiveaway
Nair, S. (2024) ‘Rainwater harvesting…’, Water Policy Review, 12(2), pp. 88–103.Harvardinitial only, year after author, single quotes
Nair, Sunita. “Rainwater Harvesting…” Water Policy Review, vol. 12, no. 2, 2024, pp. 88–103.MLAfull first name, every major word capitalised, vol. and no.
Nair, Sunita. “Rainwater Harvesting…” Water Policy Review 12, no. 2 (2024): 88–103.Chicagobare volume, year in brackets, colon before pages

Common mistakes: mixing styles; missing page numbers; citing only the abstract; not citing paraphrased ideas; wrong author order; wrong edition. Quick test: an initial only means Harvard or APA; a full first name means MLA or Chicago.
