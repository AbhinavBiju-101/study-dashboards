1The Paper at a Glance

2Q1: Reading for Inference

ToolkitSurface vs deep (what it says vs what it implies about size, scope, reliability) · Hedging ("may", "suggest", "would be needed") · The four traps (correlation as cause, cherry-picking, jargon, overgeneralisation) · Limitations (each blocks a conclusion) · Challenge the data (measured beats self-reported).

Worked model: the canteen self-service passage

Study: waste per plate fell from 78 g to 54 g (about 31%) after self-service; posters in week 7 and a price rise in week 9 were unplanned; a questionnaire found 62% of respondents believed they wasted less.

(a) Surface and deep readingSurface: average waste per plate fell from 78 g to 54 g. Deep: this compares two six-week periods in one canteen, so it shows a fall that coincided with the change, not that the change caused it. Forcing words: "Neither event was planned by us".
(b) Hedging"may reduce" and "would be needed" (also "ideally"). The authors are not confident the result generalises; they present it as suggestive, not proven.
(c) The trapCorrelation read as cause. The passage itself reports that posters went up in week 7 and the meal-plan price rose in week 9, both inside the measurement period and "not allowed for in the design", and there was no comparison canteen. Any of them could explain the fall.
(d) Two limitations, and what each prevents(1) One canteen, single term → cannot conclude it works in other canteens or across the institution. (2) Two unplanned changes → cannot separate the effect of self-service from posters or price. (Also: plates were not tracked, so beliefs cannot be matched to weights.)
(e) Stronger evidenceThe weighed measurements: they record what actually happened. The questionnaire is self-reported belief from respondents only, and could not be matched to any plate's weight.

3Q2: Definitions

RuleKnow the five types and the eight methods by name. The question names one; your answer must match it. Formal = term + class + features. Operational = what is measured + threshold. Informal = plain words, no three-part structure. Negative = what it is not. Extended = formal + 2–4 methods.

Worked model: Extract A (drip irrigation)

Extract: water is delivered through narrow tubes along the crop row; small outlets called emitters release it slowly at the base of each plant; a filter removes particles because emitters block easily; a system is efficient when more than 90% of applied water reaches the root zone.

(a) Formal, "emitter"An emitter (term) is a small outlet (class) that releases water slowly at the base of each plant in a drip system (features).
(b) Operational, "efficient system"A drip system is efficient when the percentage of applied water that reaches the root zone (what is measured) is more than 90 per cent (threshold).
(c) Informal, "drip irrigation"It is a way of watering crops by letting water trickle slowly right onto the roots of each plant. Check: no technical terms, no term-is-a-class-that frame.
(d) Name the two methods (rain gauge paragraph)By Structure: "consists of a funnel, a collecting cylinder and a graduated measuring tube". By Process: "Rain falling on the funnel is channelled into the cylinder, where the depth of the collected water is read". (The opening "instrument for measuring rainfall" is the formal definition itself.)
(e) Extend: soil moisture sensor, exactly two methodsA soil moisture sensor is an instrument that measures the amount of water held in soil. (By Function) Farmers use it to decide when to irrigate, so water is not wasted. (By Example) Common types include capacitance probes and tensiometers.

4Q3: Article Sections and the Abstract

Section, job and giveaway words
SectionJobGiveaway
Literature reviewWhat others foundnames and years: "Rao (2022) reported…"
MethodsWhat was done, so it can be repeatedprocedure, sample, timing
ResultsWhat the data showednumbers, no interpretation
DiscussionWhat the data mean"This suggests…", "This indicates…"
ConclusionAnswers the opening question; says what remains to be tested"therefore", "remains to be tested"

Worked model: hospital noise extracts

ExtractSectionIts job
(i) "Menon (2023) and Iyer (2024) both report levels…"Literature reviewShows what earlier work found, so the study's gap is clear
(ii) "Sound levels were logged at one-minute intervals…"MethodsDescribes the procedure so it could be repeated
(iii) "The mean night-time level was 52 dB(A)…"ResultsReports what the data showed, with no interpretation
(iv) "This suggests that conversation… accounts for most of the peaks."DiscussionInterprets what the results mean
(v) "…deserves the attention… remains to be tested."ConclusionAnswers the opening question and states what is still untested
Abstract in 64 words, four questions in orderWhat: This study examined night-time noise in four general hospital wards, why: far less studied than intensive care units. How: Sound was logged every minute at each bed head, 10 p.m. to 6 a.m., for fourteen nights. Found + so what: The mean was 52 dB(A) against a WHO guideline of 30, with peaks above 70 dB(A) about eleven times nightly, mostly from staff conversation. Staff routines, not equipment, should change.
Marks: all four questions, in order, within the word limit.

5Q4: Types of Review

GiveawayA registered protocol means systematic. "No quality assessment" means scoping. Justify every answer by scope, method and best-for.

Worked model: the two extracts

(a) Scoping reviewScope: broad: how much research exists, which countries, which designs. Method: reports counts and categories rather than findings and states it did not assess the quality of studies. Best for: mapping a field and identifying gaps before commissioning larger work.
(b) Systematic reviewScope: focused on a specific question. Method: protocol registered before the search; three databases; 1,420 records screened by two independent reviewers against criteria fixed in advance; exclusion reasons tabulated; flow diagram. Best for: reproducible, evidence-based answers.

6Q5: APA and IEEE Referencing

AuthorsAPAIEEE
OneRao, K.K. Rao,
TwoRao, K., & Menon, P.K. Rao and P. Menon,
ThreeRao, K., Menon, P., & Das, S.K. Rao, P. Menon, and S. Das,

Four checks every time: author order (APA surname first; IEEE initials first), year position (APA second in brackets; IEEE last), volume format (APA 14(2); IEEE vol. 14, no. 2) and pp. in IEEE. In APA, italics go on the journal or book title and the volume number; APA book titles are sentence case. Watch the comma before "and" in IEEE with three or more authors.
